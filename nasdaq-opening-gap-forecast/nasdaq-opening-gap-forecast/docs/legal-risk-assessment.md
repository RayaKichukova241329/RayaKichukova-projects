# Legal risk assessment

An anonymised summary of the group's assessment under the EU AI Act and GDPR. It was written at the proposal stage, before the final model was chosen, so it describes a simpler classifier and a slightly different gap threshold than the final XGBoost model. The legal reasoning does not depend on those details.

## The system

Each trading morning, before the U.S. market opens, the system estimates the probability that the NASDAQ-100 will gap up or down at the opening bell, based on the overnight moves of major Asian and European equity indices. It is delivered as a lightweight dashboard inside the client's internal analytics portal. Users are portfolio managers, execution traders and market-strategy analysts, who use it to size orders, adjust hedges or brief clients. They only view the dashboard or download the previous forecast, and no personal data is entered or stored.

Sector: finance and capital-markets analytics.

## Risk tier under the EU AI Act: limited risk

- **Not high-risk.** It is not one of the Annex III use cases that are automatically high-risk, such as biometrics, employment screening, credit scoring or critical infrastructure.
- **Decision support only.** Traders still make and execute the decisions, so the system has no direct effect on safety or fundamental rights.
- **Public data only.** It processes market prices, so misuse would not reveal personal information.
- **Reversible harm.** Any harm is economic and reversible, and market-forecast tools with a human in the loop are generally classed as low-to-moderate risk in the literature.

## Obligations

| Obligation | Applies? | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Transparency toward users (AI Act Art. 50) | Yes | Users must be told the output is AI-generated and how to interpret it. A dashboard notice satisfies this: "Forecast generated by an AI model trained on historical index data; past performance is not predictive of future results." |
| Technical documentation and logs | Yes | Good practice: model purpose, inputs and outputs, data sources, training periods, known failure cases and performance logs. |
| Risk-management system, conformity assessment, CE marking (Arts. 9 to 20) | No | These apply only to high-risk systems. |
| Registration in the EU AI database (Art. 60) | No | Required only for high-risk systems. |
| Human oversight requirements (Art. 14) | No | Mandated for high-risk use cases. Traders already take the final decision, so no extra mechanism is needed. |
| MiFID II Art. 17 (algorithmic trading) | To flag | If the signal were ever wired directly into an automated trading engine, kill-switches and pre-trade risk checks would apply. A dashboard-only deployment does not trigger them. |

## GDPR

The system uses only public market prices and stores no personal data, so GDPR is not engaged by the data. It would need to be revisited if user-level data, such as usage logs tied to individuals, were added.
